# San Carlos City, Pangasinan — Culture and Heritage

## Scope

Cultural and heritage profile of San Carlos City, Pangasinan: the Minor Basilica, heritage landmarks, legends and traditions, and festivals (detail in `tourism/26-09-festivals.md`).

## Summary

Basilica and plaza landmarks are documented from archived LGU pages and Wikipedia; several cultural details are single-source and the folklore below is tradition, not verified fact.

## Findings

### The Minor Basilica of Saint Dominic

- The parish church of St. Dominic de Guzman in San Carlos is the city's most significant religious heritage landmark.
- Its current site is the **third location**, with a church **completed in 1773** that was destroyed by earthquakes several times; rebuilt in 1803 it measured **89 yards long and 22 yards wide** (per old LGU text).
- **Declared a Minor Basilica by Pope Francis on July 6, 2022**, and solemnly proclaimed on **January 14, 2023**.
- History traces the Dominican presence to **1587** in Binalatongan; the town's first church tradition dates from the late 16th century. | `S1`, `S4`

### Local Legends and Traditions

- **The sunken church bell of the San Juan River** — legend that the biggest church bell in the province sank while being ferried across the San Juan River and is guarded by a mermaid (see `26-09-history.md`).
- **Name-origin folklore of barangays** — e.g., Brgy. Turac's name is said to derive from *"tucac"* (Ilocano for frog); Brgy. Pagal from *"pagod/pagal"* (Tagalog for weary) or from the generous *De Guzman* clan of "Pasoso"; Brgy. Abanon from its origin as a sitio of Agdao (from the old official site's barangay pages). | `S3`

### Festivals

- **Mango-Bamboo Festival** — the city's signature festival (launched 2001; held last week of April) promoting mango and bamboo industries. | `S6`, `S1`
