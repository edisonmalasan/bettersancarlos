## The Minor Basilica of Saint Dominic

- The parish church of St. Dominic de Guzman in San Carlos is the city's most significant religious heritage landmark.
- Its current site is the **third location**, with a church **completed in 1773** that was destroyed by earthquakes several times; rebuilt in 1803 it measured **89 yards long and 22 yards wide** (per old LGU text).
- **Declared a Minor Basilica by Pope Francis on July 6, 2022**, and solemnly proclaimed on **January 14, 2023**.
- History traces the Dominican presence to **1587** in Binalatongan; the town's first church tradition dates from the late 16th century.
- Sources: [LGU Tourism page (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20240603225004/https://www.sancarlospangasinan.gov.ph/tourism); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Carlos,_Pangasinan)

## Local Legends and Traditions

- **The sunken church bell of the San Juan River** — legend that the biggest church bell in the province sank while being ferried across the San Juan River and is guarded by a mermaid (see `26-09-history.md`).
- **Name-origin folklore of barangays** — e.g., Brgy. Turac's name is said to derive from *"tucac"* (Ilocano for frog); Brgy. Pagal from *"pagod/pagal"* (Tagalog for weary) or from the generous *De Guzman* clan of "Pasoso"; Brgy. Abanon from its origin as a sitio of Agdao (from the old official site's barangay pages).
- Sources: [Old official site — barangay pages (archived, e.g., Turac)](https://web.archive.org/web/20180514083756/http://sancarloscitypangasinan.gov.ph/index.php/turac/)

## Festivals (see also `tourism/26-09-festivals.md`)

- **Mango-Bamboo Festival** — the city's signature festival (launched 2001; held last week of April) promoting mango and bamboo industries.
