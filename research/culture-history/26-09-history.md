# San Carlos City, Pangasinan — History

## Scope

Recorded history of San Carlos City, Pangasinan (formerly Binalatongan): pre-colonial era, Spanish colonial era, independence/cityhood, and significant recent moments. Heritage sites and legends live in `culture-history/26-09-culture-heritage.md`.

## Summary

Founded as the trading port of Binalatongan, renamed San Carlos after the Palaris revolt, chartered as a city under RA 4487 (effective 1966). Core events are attested by official LGU/provincial sources and Wikipedia; pre-colonial specifics and 16th-century founding dates conflict and need scholarly verification.

### Pre-Colonial Era — Binalatongan

- The city began as **Binalatongan**, described as an early trading center (barter system) with **Chinese, Japanese, and Indian traders** along riverside settlements (e.g., present-day Brgy. Mabalbalino). People are described as having a well-developed agricultural system and wearing gold jewelry when the Spaniards arrived.
- Name origin: **"Binalatongan"** — from *balatong*, the Pangasinan term for **mung bean (mongo)**, which was abundant in the area.
- The Province of Pangasinan describes it as the capital of **Caboloan**, a historically inscribed kingdom spanning parts of present-day Pangasinan, Tarlac, Nueva Ecija, La Union, Benguet, and Zambales. | `S2`, `S3`, `S5`

### Spanish Colonial Era

- **Founding dates (conflicting):** Wikipedia infobox says founded **1578**; the LGU's archived history says the community was founded by **Fr. Francisco dela Lama, an Augustinian friar, in 1587**. Both dates lack a fully citable primary source; the conflict is documented here. | `S1`, `S3`
- **1588:** Binalatongan became a vicariate; first pastor **Padre Bernardo Navarro de Santa Catalina, O.P.** He became the first Vicar, with Fr. Juan de Castro, OP, Fr. Marcos de San Antonio, and Bro. Juan Deza, OP as assistants (per LGU tourism page). | `S3`, `S4`
- **1660 — Andrés Malong** led a major revolt against Spanish colonial rule; he is regarded as a native hero of Binalatongan.
- **1762–1763 — Juan dela Cruz Palaris** (also Pantaleon Perez; "Palaris" from his skill in *esgrima de mano*, native fencing) led a revolt against tribute and forced labor; his rebels held an independent government based in Binalatongan for about two years.
- These defeats prompted **King Carlos III of Spain** to order the town razed and renamed it **San Carlos**, honoring the king; the site was moved to its present location. | `S1`, `S2`, `S5`

### The Church and the Poblacion Relocations

- **1718**: In a minor uprising led by Juan Caragay, the church and convent were set on fire; the Población was relocated east of the San Juan River (November 4, 1718, feast of St. Charles Borromeo) and the settlement was renamed **San Carlos Binalatongan**.
- **1763**: The parish church and convent of St. Dominic burned again during Palaris's revolt; **Charles III issued a decree** relocating the site to a place called "Lucban".
- The town served as the **"Mother-house of the Province"** for the Dominican Order; the Binalatongan church was described (at its 18th-century height) as among the **largest churches in the Philippines**.
- **The San Juan River bell legend:** the great bell of the Binalatongan belfry — said to be the biggest and most sonorous in the province — was lost when the raft carrying it across the San Juan River collapsed and sank; per local legend a **mermaid** guards the bell at the bottom of the river. | `S3`, `S4`, `S5`

### Philippine Independence Era / Cityhood

- **1965**: The Municipality of San Carlos was divided; the smaller zone became the **Municipality of Basista** (created under **RA 4866**). The larger portion became the **City of San Carlos**.
- **Cityhood law:** **Republic Act No. 4487**, signed **June 19, 1965** (sponsored by Congressman Angel B. Fernandez per the old official site), ratified by residents, with cityhood effective **January 1, 1966**. | `S2`, `S5`, `S6`, `S7`

### Significant Recent Historical Moments

- **1988**: NHCP historical marker ("Lungsod ng San Carlos") installed at the city plaza — Wikipedia. | `S1`
- **2011 (April 26)**: World's largest mango pie (100 m², 2 inches thick) made by city residents and the bakers' association as part of the Mango-Bamboo Festival; total cost ₱400,000; 2,000 kg of mangoes — old official site Facts and Figures. | `S6`
- **August 22, 2024**: Fire razed the **old public market** (see `news/26-09-news-current-events.md`).
