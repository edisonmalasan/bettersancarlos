### Mango-Bamboo Festival

Core facts from the Province and the archived Tourism page (historic live URL `S6`). | `S1`, `S3`

| Attribute | Detail | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Name | **Mango-Bamboo Festival** | S1, S3 |
| Launched | **2001** | S1, S3 |
| Schedule | **Last week of April**, annually (often described as a week-long event) | S1, S3 |
| Purpose | Promote San Carlos as the major trading center for **bamboo-based products/furniture** and top producer of **carabao mangoes** and other mango varieties | S1, S3 |
| Origins | Launched by the city as an annual celebration to brand the "bamboo mango capital of the north" | S1, S3 |

### Typical Festival Programming

Historical — from the archived LGU tourism page (figures page historic live URL `S7`):

- **Float parade** — floats adorned with mango and bamboo decorations.
- **Street dancing competition** — barangay contingents compete.
- **Miss San Carlos City** — the city's prestige beauty pageant.
- **Culinary contests** — mango pie showcase, mango-based dishes.
- **Bamboo-craft competition**.
- Variety shows, entertainment, and **Laro ng Lahi** (native sports olympics).
- **Giant mango pie event** (world record bid on April 26, 2011). | `S2`, `S4`

### Other Celebrations

- **Patronal fiestas** of individual barangays (e.g., Abanon honors San Isidro Labrador on the last Saturday-Sunday of March; Turac's foundation day on Black Saturday) — documented in old official site barangay pages (historical; verify current schedules).
- **Lingayen-Dagupan Archdiocese / St. Dominic parish celebrations** — 425th Jubilee (2013) etc. (historical). | `S5`, `S2`
